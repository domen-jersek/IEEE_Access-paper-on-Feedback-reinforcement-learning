\section{The Earlier Result and Its Scope}
\label{sec:legacy}

The earlier evaluation of this pipeline compared conditioned and blind feedback on
a random split. Under conditioned feedback, the fine-scope reranking improved
generated first replies on eval by $+0.014$ (cosine $p=.012$); under blind feedback
the same reranking harmed them by $-0.038$ ($p=.017$). The reversal was confined to
the legacy Laplace lift at Team and Team$\cap$Class scope. With the prior repaired,
the blind effect was $+0.002$ ($p=.86$), and the conditioned effect was also null:
Backoff+EB gave $+0.005$ on dev ($p=.90$) and $-0.002$ on eval ($p=.29$). No cell
in that evaluation produced a confirmed calibrated gain under either protocol.

That pattern admits two readings. The first is that the judge's information decides
the sign of the effect. The second is that the evaluation allowed the feedback to
profit from procedures and reply templates that the evaluation ticket shared with
the historical base, and that the apparent benefit is partly a property of the
split. The earlier paper treated the first reading as primary and the second as a
robustness check. This article reverses the emphasis. Table~\ref{tab:overlap} shows
why: the earlier ``procedure-disjoint'' split was not procedure-disjoint under any
component-wise definition. It combined reply prefix, title and subcategory into a
single hash, so a ticket could share an identical full reply or a 300-character
reply opening with training and still pass the filter. In the canonical corpus,
12.8\,\% of its 390 evaluation tickets shared an identical full reply, 53.3\,\%
shared a reply opening, and 22.6\,\% were in the same duplicate-union component as
training. The random split was similar (14.8\,\%, 58.3\,\%, 29.1\,\%).

The correction also changes a descriptive claim: the 922-ticket figure is a
prefix count, not a full-reply count (Section~\ref{sec:data}). Using it as
evidence of identical procedures would overstate the duplication.

This article therefore keeps the earlier numbers as a documented result on the
familiar regime and asks what survives when the overlap is removed. It does not
claim that the earlier sign reversal was an artefact of the judge. It claims
something narrower and more useful: the reversal is confined to the legacy lift,
and a substantial part of the measured benefit depends on evaluation tickets that
share procedures or reply templates with history. On strict history, the blind harm
disappears and the conditioned effect is small. Section~\ref{sec:regime} reports
that result; Section~\ref{sec:repair} isolates the repair; Section~\ref{sec:expert}
adds the expert check.
